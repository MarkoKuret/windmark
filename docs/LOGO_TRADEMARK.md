### How to run a figurative search

1. **TMview** (https://www.tmdn.org/tmview/): Advanced search, field "Vienna code" (type the code or use the picker),
   Offices "EUIPO" plus the EU national offices, Nice classes 9, 28, 41, status Registered and Filed. Run one search
   per code in the table, sort by office, scan thumbnails. Then the image search with the sketch.
2. **EUIPO eSearch plus** (https://euipo.europa.eu/eSearch/): Trade marks, advanced search, "Vienna classification"
   with autocomplete; or "Image search": upload the sketch, the tool proposes Vienna codes and shows similar logos.
3. **USPTO** (https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/): use the design-code search (US codes from the table, for example
   18.15.06 buoys) combined with classes 009, 028, 041, live marks only.
4. **WIPO Article 6ter database** (https://6ter.wipo.int/): search by Vienna code to make sure the logo is not close to
   a protected state flag or an intergovernmental emblem.

## 3. Practical legal guidance (EU first, then US)

### What makes a logo safe and registrable

- **Distinctiveness (EUTMR Art. 7(1)(b)):** simple geometric shapes (a plain triangle, circle, line) are refused as
  not distinctive; EUIPO's own guideline example is a plain triangle. A creative arrangement giving its own overall
  impression is accepted. Generic sail-and-wave clip art is weak for the same reason and also crowded.
- **Descriptiveness (Art. 7(1)(c)):** a picture of what the product is (a sailboat for a sailing game) is weak; a
  figure that is not simply a sailboat (a race mark with a streak) is stronger.
- **Likelihood of confusion (Art. 8):** an earlier similar logo for identical or similar goods (games, apps, online
  game services) blocks registration on opposition and can lead to an infringement claim. Search before finalising.
- **National flags, state emblems and intergovernmental emblems (Paris Convention Art. 6ter; EUTMR Art. 7(1)(h)
  and (i)):** cannot be registered as or in a mark, including heraldic imitations; state flags are protected
  without being listed. The EU/Council of Europe circle of stars is such an emblem. Keep national flags, the EU
  stars, the Red Cross and the Olympic rings out of the logo.
- **Signal and racing flags:** the International Code of Signals flags and the racing-rules signals are standard,
  shared signals; they are not state emblems and nobody owns them as such. Using them in the game UI and as
  decoration is fine. As the logo itself they are a poor choice: generic for sailing, so not distinctive, and an
  exact signal flag (for example the swallow-tailed "A" or "B" flags) adds nothing that is ours.
- **Yacht-club burgees:** are often registered marks (see above). Do not imitate one.

### Copyright: designer and AI tools

- **Freelance designer, EU (Slovenia):** copyright arises with the designer. Economic rights pass only by **written
  contract** (ZASP Art. 80); moral rights (authorship, integrity) cannot be transferred or waived in Slovenia. An
  earlier copyright is also a ground to invalidate an EU trade mark (EUTMR Art. 60(2)(c)), so an unassigned logo is a
  real weakness, not a formality.
- **Freelance designer, US:** a logo is not one of the nine "work made for hire" categories for contractors, so a
  "work for hire" clause alone does not work; use a **signed written assignment** (17 U.S.C. § 204(a)). The author can
  terminate an assignment after 35 years (§ 203), a small practical risk.
- **AI-generated images, EU:** copyright needs a human "author's own intellectual creation" (CJEU Infopaq, Painer,
  Cofemel). The Prague Municipal Court (10 C 13/2023) refused protection for a DALL-E image made from a prompt.
- **AI-generated images, US:** the D.C. Circuit (Thaler v. Perlmutter, 18 March 2025) confirmed human authorship is
  required; the Copyright Office's January 2025 report says prompts alone are not enough, human selection and
  modification can be.
- **Effect on trademarks:** a trade mark does not need an author, so an AI-made logo can still be registered if it is
  distinctive. But nobody owns copyright in it, so it cannot be enforced as artwork (merch copies, use in unrelated
  fields), it may resemble images the tool was trained on, and some tools' terms limit use. Recommendation: AI only
  for mood boards; the final mark drawn by a human, with dated sketches and working files kept.

### What to require from a freelance designer

1. Written, signed **assignment of all economic rights**: exclusive, worldwide, for the full term, all media and
   uses (game, stores, marketing, merchandise, trade-mark filing), with the right to modify, adapt and sublicense.
2. **Consent to adaptations** and an agreement that no designer credit is required on the logo itself (moral rights
   stay with the author under Slovenian law, so agree how they are exercised).
3. **Originality warranty**: own work, no stock, no third-party elements, no AI output unless disclosed; indemnity if
   this proves untrue.
4. **Disclosure** of every font and tool used, with licences.
5. **Deliverables**: vector masters (SVG, PDF, AI or Affinity), colour, one-colour and reversed versions, a 16 to 32
   px icon version, colour values, clear-space and minimum-size notes, and the working files and sketches.
6. **No challenge and cooperation**: the designer will not oppose or attack our trade-mark filings and will sign
   any further confirmation needed by an office.
7. **Confidentiality** until the public reveal; portfolio use only after launch.
8. Rights transfer on payment; Slovenian law. If hiring through a marketplace, check that its terms transfer IP to
   the buyer.

### Font licence for the wordmark (Barlow)

- Barlow is under the SIL Open Font License 1.1; its `OFL.txt` declares **no Reserved Font Name**.
- The OFL FAQ (1.1) says fonts may be used to create logos without further permission, and the author of the
  artwork remains its copyright holder; the licence does not restrict the artwork. So a logo set in Barlow can be
  used and filed as a trade mark.
- Limits: our mark gives us no rights over the font itself, and an unmodified Barlow wordmark adds little
  distinctiveness beyond the word mark. A designer should customise the letterforms (cuts, joins, a custom W).

Order: **file the word mark first** (broadest, survives any logo change). File the logo later, only when it is
final and in use, and only if budget allows. An EU filing gives 6 months' priority (Paris Convention) for a US
filing of the same mark. A figurative filing protects the exact drawing submitted (colour only if filed in colour;
black and white is usual).

### Where a trademark lawyer is worth paying for

- An opinion on the St. Joe Co. US class 41 marks against our class 41 wording (online game services, esports
  competitions) before any US filing or US marketing push.
- A clearance search and opinion on the final logo before it goes on the Steam page (a fixed-fee search report
  costs far less than a rebrand after launch).
- The US filing itself (required anyway for a non-US applicant).
- Drafting or checking the designer assignment if the designer is outside Slovenia.

## 4. Design guardrails for the logo designer

1. The symbol must be ours: a race mark, a course or a track, drawn so it is not just a sailboat, a sail triangle
   or a wave.
2. No plain geometric shape as the whole mark (a lone triangle, circle or chevron).
3. No sail triangles or boat silhouettes as the main element (World Sailing, Musto, hundreds of clubs).
4. No feathered arrow, weathervane, compass rose or compass points; no script lettering; never "WindMark" in camel
   case (WindMark Beach).
5. No trophy or ewer shape (America's Cup); no circle split by a horizon line (North Sails).
6. No wind-turbine rotor or three-blade shape.
7. A W built from a zigzag must read as a course between marks, not as a plain letter; no doubled "WW".
8. A pennant or burgee may not carry a cross, crown, star, anchor or initials, and must not copy a signal flag.
9. No national flag, flag colours arranged as a flag, EU stars, Red Cross or Olympic rings.
10. Works in one colour, reversed, and at 16 px (Steam icon, favicon, app icon).
11. Wordmark in customised Barlow (or another OFL font with the licence file kept); list every font used.
12. No AI-generated final artwork; AI only for mood boards, and say so.
13. Deliver vectors, working files and dated sketches; sign the assignment before the final files change hands.
14. Before sign-off, run the TMview image search and the Vienna-code searches above on the finalist.
