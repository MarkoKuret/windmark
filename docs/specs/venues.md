# Venues

A venue is a real sailing area with its own character: the wind it usually gets, how that wind shifts and gusts,
the current, and the sea. The character lives in the venue file's `conditions` (`data_format.md` §6.7) and is told
to the player before a race by a short standard **briefing** on the quick race card (`menus.md` §1.3). The player
picks one of three weather modes (`race.md` §2.1): **Local** (the venue's own weather, full briefing), **Random** (a
wider surprise day, no briefing at all) or **Custom** (own wind, shift and gust picks; briefing without the Tip). The race
layer turns the conditions and the race seed into the per-race wind, current and sea settings (`race.md` §2.1), so
the core needs no new physics: every venue is the same wind (`wind.md`) and sea (`sea.md`) with other numbers.

Venue files are generated by `tools/venuegen` from a coastline source (§3) and a hand-owned conditions file
(WM-105); the generated grids and land are never hand-edited.

## 4. Performance budget

Venues must cost no more than `practice_waters` does today: in the audit the venue is 3 to 5 draws and about 80k
triangles on a race camera, and removing it saves no measurable GPU time (`docs/PERFORMANCE.md`, "Venue" and
"Removing X saves"). Draw calls are the cost on the target machines, so the budget caps draws first. Each venue task
measures headless against `practice_waters` (draw calls, frame time, core tick time) and must show no measurable
regression.

### 4.1 Physics and data (the core)

| Item | Cap | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Land polygons (`data_format.md` §6.5) | at most 4000 vertices in total, at most 1500 per polygon; simplified (Douglas-Peucker) with a 5 m tolerance inside `bounds`, coarser outside (20 m unless the source says otherwise, §5.2); clipped to `bounds` grown by 1 km | collision and AI land checks scale with nearby edges |
| Obstacles | at most 64 | |
| `wind_field` grid | at most 96 × 96 nodes, spacing at least 50 m | 8 sectors: at most 0.6 MB in memory, 75 KB per texture upload |
| `current_field`, `exposure` grids | at most 128 × 128 nodes each, spacing at least 50 m | |
| Venue file | at most 3 MB of JSON; loads in under 100 ms on the dev Mac | one load per race |

- **Lookups cost the same as today.** Wind, current and exposure use the one bilinear sampler of `wind.md` §8.1:
  constant cost per query whatever the grid size, so a big venue costs what `practice_waters` costs. No lookup may loop
  over land, polygons or grid rows.
- **Land queries cost what open water costs.** Each land polygon gets an edge grid when the venue loads (square
  cells over its box, about two per edge, at most 256 a side, plus a per-row edge list and its bounding circle). The
  per-tick land queries (rule 19's obstruction cull and gap, the AI's land check, the spawn search) visit only the
  cells near the boat and grow the search radius only until the nearest edge is found, so their cost follows the
  edges near the boat, not the coastline's length. Results are bit for bit those of the full scans (the rounding
  of a distance is far below the 1 m slack of the search); the collision band index (`collisions.md` §3.4) is the
  same idea.
- **No new per-tick or per-frame work.** The conditions are resolved once when the race is created (`race.md`
  §2.1); the trend and the tide are the existing terms of `wind.md` §3 and §7 (a tide with a field already costs one
  cosine per current query, as `practice_waters` would with `tide_scale > 0`; an implementation may cache `tide(t)` per
  tick like `global_from`). The briefing is computed in the menu when a row changes, never in a race.
- The land-factor texture on the water is uploaded only when the wind's sector pair changes, as today
  (`water_render.md` §4.3); the larger grid only makes that rare upload bigger.

### 4.2 Drawn land (the front end)

| Item | Cap |
| --- | --- |
| Materials | one shared land material for every land and scenery mesh of a venue (the existing land shader: vertex colour and value noise, no unique terrain textures), plus the existing water |
| Draw calls, main pass | at most 8 for the venue (terrain chunks, horizon ridge, scenery), at every tier |
| Draw calls, shadow pass | at most 2 (near terrain chunks only) on M and H; none on L |
| Terrain | at most 4 chunks (venuegen writes 2 near chunks within 1.5 km of `bounds`, west and east, which cast shadows, and 2 far ones), one static mesh each whose levels of detail are built into it by distance from the racing water (where the cameras are): 5 m cells at the shore within 500 m and 10 m to 1.5 km (LOD0), 20 m to 5 km (LOD1), 80 m beyond (LOD2); the relief refines down to 10 m (the elevation model has 30 m posts) while it misses the height by more than about 0.45° seen from the water. No run-time LOD switching, so no popping |
| Horizon ridge | far mountains and islands as one low mesh, at most 10k triangles, no shadows |
| Scenery (trees, buildings) | at most 2 MultiMesh draws, culled beyond 3 km |
| Triangles drawn | at most 120k on a race camera at tier M (today about 80k) |
| Drawn coastline | at most 20k vertices in total: within the land margin it is the physics land itself (§5.2), 10 m tolerance beyond to 5 km from `bounds`, 50 m further out |
| Video memory | at most 32 MB per venue |
| Placed sounds (`audio.md` §7) | at most 8 sources |

The drawn land comes from the same coastline and terrain sources as the physics land; within the land margin its
waterline is the physics land's (the terrain is shaped on the signed distance to it, §5.7), so the shore drawn inside
`bounds` stays within 5 m of the physics land (a venuegen test checks it 5 m either side) and boats never visibly hit
water or pass through rock. Land polygons wider than 3 km are never drawn from their polygon (the island path of
WM-016.21 tessellates a 5 m grid over a polygon's box): such a venue needs a terrain file.

## 5. The venue generator (`tools/venuegen`)

`venuegen` turns a hand-owned **venue source file** and its coastline inputs into the venue file of
`data_format.md` §6. Everything in the generated grids and land comes from the land shape and the source's numbers;
nothing is edited by hand afterwards (the tool's tests regenerate each `data/venues/<id>.venue.json` from its source and
compares). The model below is deliberately simple and deterministic (NumPy, no randomness); its constants are
defaults that a venue may override in its source, and the venue tasks tune them against a test race.

### 5.1 Source file (`windmark.venuesrc/1`)

`data/venue_sources/<id>.venuesrc.json`, JSON, SI units and radians like the venue file (`data_format.md` §1).
Raw coastline downloads live in `data/venue_sources/raw/` (not committed, `.gitignore`); the README of the tool says
where each source of §3 comes from.

| key | meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `schema` | `"windmark.venuesrc/1"` |
| `id`, `name`, `version`, `origin`, `bounds`, `fetch`, `exposure_default` | copied to the venue header (`data_format.md` §6.1); `version` is bumped by hand on every change |
| `coastline` | list of inputs (below); empty or absent: open water |
| `land_height` | m [20]: height of land with no height of its own (drives the wind effects, §5.3) |
| `obstacles` | copied as they are (`data_format.md` §6.5) and validated |
| `current` | the current field description (§5.4); absent: no `current_field` |
| `exposure` | `{ "from": rad, ... }`: the typical wind direction the exposure grid is made for [`conditions.wind.from`, else 0], plus §5.5 constants; `false`: no exposure grid |
| `wind` | §5.3 constants; `"wind_field": false` leaves the field out |
| `simplify` | `inside` [5 m], `outside` [20 m], `outside_max` [160 m], `min_area` [20 m²], `land_margin` [1000 m], `field_margin` [3000 m], `field_tolerance` [10 m] |
| `grids` | `margin` [1000 m]: every grid covers `bounds` grown by this; `wind`, `current`, `exposure`: the wanted spacing [100 m], coarsened to a multiple of 10 m when the caps of §4.1 need it |
| `conditions`, `briefing` | copied to the venue file (`data_format.md` §6.7, §6.8) after the checks there |
| `terrain` | the drawn terrain and the elevation model (§5.7); a string is only free text for the `source` note |
| `note` | free text added to the venue's `source` note |

A `coastline` input is one of
- `{"frame": "local", "role": "land", "polygons": [{"id", "height", "points"}]}`: polygons in local metres (our own
  shapes, e.g. the Practice island);
- `{"path": "raw/...", "frame": "lonlat" | "local", "role": ..., "height": m}`: a GeoJSON file (Polygon,
  MultiPolygon, LineString, MultiLineString) or an ESRI shapefile (`.shp` polygons or polylines; a `.prj`, when
  present, must be geographic: projected data is reprojected to EPSG:4326 first). Lon/lat is projected to the
  local east/north metres of the tangent plane at `origin` (WGS84).

`role`: `land` (polygons are land; their holes are dropped), `water` (polygons are water, e.g. a lake; land is
outside them, their holes are islands), `shore_left` / `shore_right` (open lines with the land on that side of
their direction; they must run past the clip box at both ends). Each input may carry `source`, `version`,
`downloaded` (date) and `licence`, which make up the venue's `source` note.

### 5.2 Land

The inputs are clipped to `bounds` grown by `land_margin` (the written land) and, separately, by `field_margin`
(the land the grids are computed from, so a shore beyond the written land still shelters the water). Clipping keeps
the land on the left of every path and closes the pieces along the clip box edge, counter-clockwise, so the result
is simple counter-clockwise polygons; a water body wholly inside the box is handled by splitting the box through it.
The written land is simplified with Douglas-Peucker at `inside` within `bounds` and `outside` beyond (points on the
clip box edge are kept), checked simple and counter-clockwise on the binary32 values the core reads (the tolerance is
halved for a polygon until it is), and pieces under `min_area` are dropped (rocks are obstacles). Over the vertex caps
of §4.1, `outside` is doubled up to `outside_max`; still over, `venuegen` refuses (shrink `bounds` or split the
land). Land ids come from the input polygon ids, else `land_1`, `land_2`, ... in output order. Big land polygons
(a lake's whole surroundings, a mainland) stay whole: the core's land queries go through a per-polygon edge grid
(WM-115), and cutting the land into tiles measured slower with it. The land is rasterised on a 25 m work grid
(`wind.work_resolution`) with each polygon's height, or with the elevation model's where the source has one (§5.7).

### 5.3 Wind field

For each of the 8 sectors (`data_format.md` §6.2), with `u` the downwind unit vector:

1. **Wake.** Marching downwind row by row, land of height `h` sets a speed deficit
   `S = shadow_max · h / (h + shadow_h_ref)` [0.5, 15 m]; behind the land `S` decays as `exp(−s / L)` with
   `L = wake_per_height · h` [30, clamped to 400 to 6000 m] and spreads sideways like a plane wake (diffusion with
   `wake_diffusivity` [45 m]: width `sqrt(2 K s)`), so a rock's wake fades in a few hundred metres and a mountain's
   lasts kilometres.
2. **Shore bending.** The land mask blurred with `σ_b = bend_per_height · h̄` [6, clamped to 150 to 1000 m; `h̄`
   the mean land height] gives the seaward shore normal `n` and a shore strength `b` (1 at a long straight shore,
   fading over `σ_b`). Where the wind blows toward the shore, a share `onshore_block · b · h / (h + 15 m)` [0.5] of
   its shoreward component is removed, so it turns to run along the shore (and slows close to it: the same share
   times `b` again); where it blows off the shore, a share `offshore_turn · b` [0.5] of its along-shore component is
   removed, so it leaves the shore more square to it. The offset `d` is the turn, at most `max_bend` [0.5 rad].
3. **Coastal convergence.** With the land mask blurred over `coriolis_length` [1500 m], wind blowing along a shore
   with the land on its right (looking downwind; on its left in the southern hemisphere, from `origin.lat`) is
   stronger and with the land on its left weaker, by up to `coriolis` [8 %].
4. `f = (1 − S) · blocking · convergence`, clamped to [0, 1.5]; `f` and `d` fade to `(1, 0)` over the outer
   `taper` [500 m] of the grid, and the border nodes are exactly `(1, 0)`.

### 5.4 Current field

`current: {"to": rad, "speed": m/s, "shore_length", "shore_floor", "bank_blur", "banks": [{"id", "points",
"factor"}]}`: a stream of `speed` toward `to` (for a tide, the spring-tide flood at tide factor 1), slowed near the
shore to `shore_floor` [0.2] of it and recovering as `1 − exp(−d / shore_length)` [400 m] with the distance `d` to
land; steered along the shore (the shoreward component removed with the shore strength of §5.3 at
`σ = shore_length`, so a stream meeting land head-on also slows); over each bank polygon multiplied by its `factor`
(edges softened over `bank_blur` [100 m]); zero on land. A uniform current of `conditions.current` needs no field.
`ref_speed` follows `data_format.md` §6.3.

### 5.5 Exposure

For the typical wind direction (§5.1), the open-water fetch upwind of each node is found along 61 directions over
`±spread` [90°] around it (rays stop at land; beyond the work raster the water is open; capped at the header
`fetch`), and `E = Σ cos²(δ) · sqrt(F(δ) / fetch) / Σ cos²(δ)` (the wind-sea height grows with the square root of
fetch, `sea.md` §2). Near the shore `E` is multiplied by `1 − exp(−d / fade_length)` [100 m, a few wavelengths],
and land nodes are 0. So the windward side of an island sees the full sea and its lee is flat, fading back over the
open water.

### 5.6 Output and checks

Numbers are written rounded (land to 0.1 m, `f`, `d`, current to 4 decimals, exposure to 3) and the file is
checked against every rule of `data_format.md` §6 and the caps of §4.1 before it is written (`--check` validates
any venue file). `venuegen` prints the land vertex count and any dropped piece or raised tolerance.

### 5.7 Terrain

`terrain: {"dem": [paths], "source", "version", "downloaded", "licence", "water_level", ...}` gives the venue an
elevation model (NASADEM GeoTIFF tiles, §3, kept in `raw/` like the coastline) and the drawn terrain file
(`data_format.md` §6.10). The constants (defaults in `windmark_venuegen/terrain.py`) may be overridden in the block.

- **Wind heights.** Every land cell of the work raster takes the DEM height above `water_level` (the DEM height of
  the still water: a lake's level, 0 at sea), clamped to [`wind_height_min` 2 m, `wind_height_max` 3000 m], instead
  of its polygon's `height` (which stays the fallback where the DEM has no data, and for venues without a DEM). A
  valley wind that flows between mountains rather than over them caps `wind_height_max` (Garda: 200 m).
- **Drawn height.** With `s` the signed distance to the coastline (positive inland; the physics land within the land
  margin, blended over 300 m into the drawn coastline beyond, §4.2) and `D` the DEM above the water: under water
  `max(under_slope · s, −depth)` [0.35, 8 m]; on land a shore ramp `min(shore_slope · s, shore_cap)` [0.12, 2.5 m]
  blended into `max(D, ramp)` over `shore_band` [80 m]. The waterline is exactly `s = 0`. Without `dem` the land
  rises to its polygon's height over `hill_width` [400 m] inland.
- **Mesh.** An adaptive quadtree over `bounds` grown by `margin` [6000 m] on a 5 m lattice, 640 m roots: a cell
  splits while it holds the waterline or misses the drawn height by more than `tolerance + tolerance_per_km · d`
  [1 m, 8 m/km], `d` the distance from the racing water, down to the cells of §4.2; a 2:1 balance and fans at the
  T-junctions keep it crack-free; cells deep under water are dropped; a 30 m skirt hangs from the outer edge. Over
  `triangles` [110k] both the tolerance and the fine zones scale by 1.25 until it fits.
- **Ridge.** The DEM on a grid out to `ridge` [22 km] from the centre of `bounds`, at the finest of 320, 640, 1280 or
  2560 m that fits `ridge_triangles` [10k], skipping the terrain box and open water.
- **Ground grid.** The drawn height every `ground_spacing` [10 m] over `bounds` grown by `near`, for the cameras.

Raw downloads stay out of the repo; `windmark_venuegen.prep` turns them into venuegen inputs without GDAL (`utm`:
a UTM shapefile to lon/lat GeoJSON; `dem-water`: a DEM's flattened water body as a polygon; `splice`: two water
polygons joined at a latitude). The tool README lists the commands per venue.
